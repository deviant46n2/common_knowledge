# Institutional Design — Deferred Questions

**Status:** Deliberately deferred; not a current workstream  
**Related:** [Founding Principles](../FOUNDING_PRINCIPLES.md)

Common Knowledge has founding commitments concerning public benefit, political independence, openness with justified protections, fair work, contributor agency, and resistance to private capture. Those commitments guide current work.

A detailed legal structure, membership model, board design, voting system, compensation formula, or institutional constitution is not needed to build and test the first software slice. Designing these structures without a working product or a community would be premature.

## What remains important now

- Do not make irreversible legal, licensing, funding, or ownership commitments without explicit review.
- Do not collect sensitive data or accept substantial assets without appropriate safeguards.
- Be transparent about the project's early, founder-led status.
- Treat contributors fairly and make expectations clear.
- Keep product and research decisions open to criticism and revision.

## When to revisit

Return to institutional design when practical needs arise—for example, before accepting significant funding, employing people, holding shared assets, operating a public service with sensitive data, or asking a community to assume ongoing stewardship.

At that point, research real organizations and obtain qualified legal advice where needed. People who use and contribute to Common Knowledge should help shape the arrangements. This document is a reminder to revisit the questions, not a proposal for how to answer them.
