# Transparency

**Status:** Initial founder-led operating commitments; not an audited financial report, independent oversight system, or completed governance framework.

Common Knowledge aims to build open infrastructure for humanity's shared knowledge. The way the project is developed should be consistent with that aim. Transparency begins with the founder and applies to project work, decisions, resources, and limitations.

## What we will make visible

### Development

- Source code, issues, pull requests, design proposals, and roadmap changes should be public by default.
- Significant technical and product decisions should include the problem, alternatives considered, evidence, rationale, trade-offs, and known uncertainties.
- Failed experiments, corrections, and material changes in direction should remain discoverable.
- Project updates should distinguish what is proposed, implemented, tested, independently user-tested, and released.
- Work performed with AI assistance should not be presented as independent human research or review. Material AI assistance and verification limits should be disclosed where relevant.

### Founder and decision-making

While Common Knowledge is founder-led, that fact must be explicit. The founder should:

- Explain significant decisions and changes in direction.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest and material affiliations.
- Distinguish personal opinions from project positions.
- Disclose material commitments made on behalf of the project.
- Invite criticism and preserve substantive disagreement rather than implying consensus.
- Correct public statements when they are materially wrong or incomplete.

These commitments do not create independent oversight or remove the founder's current authority. They make the present situation visible while the project is small.

### Money and resources

If the project receives or spends money, it should publish a project-level record of:

- Funding received, including source, date or reporting period, amount, and any material restrictions or conditions.
- Project expenditures, with date or reporting period, amount, category, recipient or vendor where disclosure is lawful and safe, and a short purpose.
- Compensation, fees, reimbursements, and other payments to the founder, insiders, contractors, and related parties.
- Material in-kind contributions or donated services where they affect project capacity or create a relevant conflict.
- Available balances and financial commitments, when the project has funds or liabilities to report.

Reports should state the reporting period, currency, accounting basis, and known omissions. Corrections should be dated and explained. If there is no project income or spending in a period, say so rather than implying that a financial operation exists.

At this early stage, public reporting is a founder commitment, not an audited account. We must not imply independent verification, fiduciary oversight, or a formal nonprofit financial-control system that does not exist.

### Funding independence

Funding must not purchase control over research conclusions, editorial decisions, technical standards, appointments, or the project's mission. Material donor conditions and conflicts should be disclosed before accepting support where possible.

The project should not accept funding whose conditions are incompatible with its public-benefit purpose or whose risks cannot be responsibly assessed. The practical rules for accepting, holding, and spending funds will need to mature before significant funds or shared assets are involved.

## Reporting rhythm

While the project remains small:

- Maintain a public record of significant decisions as they occur.
- Publish a concise monthly project update covering progress, changes, blockers, and next priorities.
- Publish a project financial summary at least quarterly if money has been received, held, or spent; publish a clear zero-activity statement when useful.
- Publish material corrections or incident disclosures promptly, subject to legitimate safety, privacy, and legal constraints.

The format can remain simple: dated Markdown files in the repository are sufficient at this stage.

## What transparency does not mean

Transparency does not justify publishing:

- Personal addresses, private contact details, bank or payment credentials, or identity documents.
- Private information about contributors or other people without consent.
- Security credentials, exploitable vulnerability details before responsible disclosure, or information that would create a foreseeable safety risk.
- Confidential material entrusted to the project under legitimate restrictions.
- Donor or vendor personal data where disclosure is not lawful or necessary; material funding relationships and conflicts should still be disclosed in a safe form.

When information must be withheld, explain the category and reason where doing so is safe and lawful. Do not use privacy as a blanket excuse for hiding project decisions or financial flows.

## Accountability and future development

Publishing information is not the same as being accountable. As contributors and users join, they should help determine practical mechanisms for questioning decisions, reviewing conflicts, correcting records, and changing who holds authority.

We will not claim that Common Knowledge is capture-proof. We will work toward visible, limited, distributed, contestable authority and independent preservation. Formal oversight, financial review, and governance mechanisms should be developed with a real community and appropriate professional advice before they are represented as existing safeguards.

## Current status

Common Knowledge is an early, founder-led project. This document records intended practices; it is not evidence that all practices have already been implemented. Any gaps between commitment and practice should be acknowledged and corrected publicly.
