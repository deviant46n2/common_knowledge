# Common Knowledge

**Open infrastructure for humanity's shared knowledge.**

Common Knowledge is an early public-benefit, open-source project exploring how people and independent groups can create, inspect, challenge, exchange, and preserve shared knowledge.

The long-term ambition is a durable, evidence-linked commons that remains understandable, portable, and useful across institutions, platforms, generations, and future AI systems. No single organization should need to control it.

That is an ambition, not a description of what exists today. The immediate task is to build a small, useful piece of software that developers can run, inspect, challenge, and improve.

## Why this matters

Knowledge is cumulative, but the records that support it are scattered across papers, datasets, archives, databases, websites, software, and individual expertise. It can be difficult to trace a statement back to its evidence, understand its scope and uncertainty, see how it changed, or reuse it outside the system where it was created.

Common Knowledge aims to make those relationships easier to inspect and build upon.

It should be a **knowledge authority, not a truth authority**: a place and set of tools for examining evidence and methods, not an institution that decrees what everyone must believe.

## What we're building toward

- **Evidence-linked knowledge:** statements can be traced to sources, methods, observations, and contributors.
- **Inspectable disagreement:** competing assessments, limitations, context, and corrections remain visible.
- **Interoperability:** independent people and organizations can exchange and build on records.
- **Resilience:** knowledge can be exported, copied, preserved, and maintained without one privileged host.
- **Human–AI collaboration:** people and AI systems can help discover, organize, and examine knowledge with provenance and accountability.
- **Public-interest stewardship:** the project aims to resist private capture and excessive concentration of control.

## How we're beginning

Our first intended community is developers and open-source contributors interested in knowledge representation, provenance, interoperability, digital preservation, security, data portability, and public-interest infrastructure.

The first product hypothesis is a small, local-first reference implementation with a developer-friendly workflow. It should let someone create, inspect, validate, and export a knowledge record containing a scoped statement, sources, precise evidence references, separate assessments, provenance, and revision history.

The first release does not need production federation. It should, however, make independent operation and preservation possible rather than making a central Common Knowledge service the only place records can exist.

We will reuse existing standards and projects wherever practical. We will not invent a universal ontology, central identity authority, or new protocol without demonstrating a real need.

## Founding commitments

- Public benefit rather than private extraction.
- Freedom of inquiry and political independence.
- Evidence, transparency, correction, and intellectual honesty.
- Openness by default, with justified protections for privacy, consent, safety, and community rights.
- Fair treatment of contributors and reasonable compensation when resources permit.
- Resistance to private capture and excessive concentration of control.
- Interoperability, independent preservation, and a mission that can outlast its founders.

These are guiding commitments, not a completed constitution. Detailed governance should be developed with the community that forms around the work. Transparency, however, begins immediately: the project is founder-led today, and its development decisions and project finances should be made visible from the beginning.

## Get involved

The repository is the current public home for development. At this early stage, useful contributions include:

- Critiquing the product hypothesis and technical direction.
- Identifying relevant standards, projects, and integration opportunities.
- Sharing concrete examples of evidence-tracing and preservation problems.
- Reviewing proposed data models and architecture decisions.
- Testing the developer workflow as it becomes runnable.

We want people to challenge the design, propose alternatives, and build compatible tools—not simply implement decisions made by the founder.

No project software or contributor license has been selected yet. Licensing remains an explicit decision; please open an issue before relying on reuse or contribution terms.

## The long view

The knowledge must outlive the systems that helped create it.

**A home for humanity's shared knowledge, built by everyone, for everyone.**
