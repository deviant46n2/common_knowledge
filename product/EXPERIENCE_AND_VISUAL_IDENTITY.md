# Experience Principles and Visual Direction

- **Status:** Provisional direction for the knowledge-system demonstrator
- **Date:** 26 November 2026

## Design character

The experience should feel like a careful reference work joined to an inspectable research notebook: calm, readable, precise, and open to correction.

It should not feel like a generic SaaS dashboard, a political fact-checking scoreboard, or an AI chatbot presenting confident answers.

## Core experience

The central object is a knowledge record. A reader should be able to move naturally between:

1. **The statement** — what is being said, with definitions and scope.
2. **The evidence** — the sources, passages, datasets, observations, or results relevant to it.
3. **The assessment** — how the evidence has been interpreted, by whom, using what method, with what limitations.
4. **The discussion** — meaningful objections, alternative interpretations, and unresolved questions.
5. **The history** — prior versions, corrections, and reasons for changes.

Do not collapse these layers into a single badge or score.

## Visual direction

Use an editorial, instrument-like visual language:
- strong readable typography and clear hierarchy;
- restrained color and generous whitespace;
- visible citations, source metadata, and provenance;
- clear distinctions between source material, interpretation, and commentary;
- timelines and relationship views only where they clarify the record;
- accessible contrast, keyboard navigation, and responsive layouts.

Avoid:
- gamified reputation or consensus meters;
- simplistic true/false color coding for complex questions;
- decorative AI motifs, glowing gradients, or excessive animation;
- dense metadata walls that obscure the actual evidence;
- UI that implies the system has certified reality.

## Language and trust

Use precise language such as:
- "This source reports..."
- "The record's assessment is..."
- "This evidence supports the statement within the stated scope."
- "A conflicting source or interpretation is recorded here."
- "This has not been independently checked."

Avoid claims such as:
- "Common Knowledge proves..."
- "AI verified the truth."
- "The community has decided this is true."
- "No evidence exists" when the actual observation is only that none has been found in the searched material.

## Brand relationship

The software should be recognizably part of Common Knowledge but useful on its own. Explain the task before the mission. Do not create a separate product brand or full design system before testing the workflow.

## Validation

Before treating the visual direction as established, put it in front of people using a working record view. Test whether they can tell source from assessment, find the relevant evidence, understand uncertainty, and recognize what changed.
