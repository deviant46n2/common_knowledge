# protocols.io — Project Profile

- **Category:** Protocol and methods authoring, collaboration, and publication
- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Initial profile; current transition information is time-sensitive
- **Primary site:** https://www.protocols.io/

## What it is

protocols.io is a platform for developing, organizing, versioning, sharing, and running detailed research methods. Its documentation describes collaborative editing, protocol histories, interactive and runnable methods, and tools for recording experimental details such as reagents, parameters, and media.

The service is relevant to the methods layer of research: a paper may describe a procedure at a high level, while a structured protocol records the operational steps needed to reproduce it.

## Material transition affecting OSF users

As of the November 2026 snapshot, protocols.io is actively promoting migration for researchers whose methods, notes, and files are in OSF Projects. Its migration page states that OSF stopped allowing new projects on 16 November 2026 and that existing projects are scheduled to become read-only on 19 February 2027. protocols.io is marketing itself as one destination for active methods and project materials.

These dates and migration offers are time-sensitive and should be rechecked before anyone acts on them. The migration page is also a vendor's description of its own service and offer, not an independent comparison of alternatives.

## Strengths and potentially relevant design choices

- Treats protocols as structured, evolving research objects rather than static prose buried in papers.
- Supports version history and collaborative method development.
- Can make detailed methods more discoverable and reusable.
- The OSF transition highlights the importance of migration paths and continuity when shared infrastructure changes direction.

## Limitations and open questions

- The platform's feature set, pricing, access controls, export formats, and institutional offerings need a fuller review.
- The current migration messaging is commercial positioning; alternatives and user outcomes should be investigated.
- A protocol record alone does not establish that a method is valid, suitable for a new context, or successfully reproduced.
- Long-term preservation, exit options, and export completeness require independent verification.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

The case reinforces two requirements for any future Commons infrastructure: research records should be portable, and the loss or transition of one service should not strand a community's methods and project history. Common Knowledge should avoid becoming a single point of failure for protocols or project records.
