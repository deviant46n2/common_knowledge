# Project Profile: medRxiv

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://www.medrxiv.org/
- **Operator:** openRxiv
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

medRxiv is a free online archive for complete but unpublished manuscripts in medical, clinical, and related health sciences. Its own documentation prominently warns that preprints have not been peer reviewed and should not guide clinical practice or health behavior.

## Main capabilities

- Hosts eligible medical and health-sciences preprints.
- Screens submissions for plagiarism, scope, ethics oversight, clinical trial registration, potentially identifying participant information, and potential risks to public health.
- States that screening typically takes two to four days, though it can take longer.
- No submission fee is charged.
- Provides pathways for transfer or submission to participating journals and review services.

## Technical and operational model

medRxiv demonstrates why openness needs domain-sensitive safeguards. Preprints can accelerate communication, but medical claims may be misinterpreted as clinical advice. The server's screening and warning labels reduce some risks but do not constitute peer review or clinical validation.

## Strengths

- Free submission and rapid dissemination.
- Explicit screening for ethical, privacy, and potential public-health concerns.
- Clear warnings that preprints are not peer reviewed and should not direct clinical decisions.
- Links into subsequent journal and review workflows.

## Limitations and risks

- Screening cannot guarantee scientific correctness or eliminate all potential harms.
- Preprints may be amplified or misreported before validation.
- Health research requires careful handling of participant privacy and public communication.
- Funding and governance require separate investigation.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

medRxiv is an important case for Common Knowledge's principle of openness with justified protections. Any cross-domain knowledge platform must distinguish preliminary research from validated guidance and apply stricter safeguards where public harm is plausible.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
