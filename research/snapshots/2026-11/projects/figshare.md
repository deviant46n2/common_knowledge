# Figshare — Project Profile

- **Category:** Research-output repository service and institutional repository platform
- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Initial profile; commercial terms and institutional contracts need independent review
- **Primary site:** https://figshare.com/

## What it is

Figshare is a research-output repository service operated by Digital Science. It supports publication and sharing of datasets and other research outputs, with item metadata, persistent identifiers, versioning, APIs, and institutional repository products.

Its public policy describes free public access to published content, API-based access subject to the item's license, and CC0 as the default license for metadata. Dataset licenses can be selected by depositors, including CC0 and CC BY.

## Preservation and continuity

Figshare's public policy describes automated backups to Amazon S3, versioning, and preservation arrangements. Its security and stability documentation distinguishes the general Figshare researcher service from institutional products: institutional customers can integrate their preferred preservation systems, and Figshare states that it does not itself provide preservation services for institutional clients.

This distinction matters. A marketing statement about long-term preservation should not be generalized across all Figshare deployments or treated as a substitute for reading the contract, preservation arrangement, and exit terms applicable to a particular institution.

## Strengths

- A relatively accessible way to publish diverse research outputs.
- APIs and persistent identifiers support discovery and programmatic access.
- Versioning supports updates to published records.
- Public content can be downloaded without an account, subject to licenses and access conditions.
- Institutional deployments can be configured and integrated with other systems.

## Limitations and open questions

- Figshare is commercially operated; governance and continuity differ from a member-governed or nonprofit community project.
- Institutional products and the public researcher service may have materially different preservation, curation, and contractual arrangements.
- The service's default CC0 dataset license may not suit every dataset or contributor expectation; depositors must choose deliberately.
- Public policy pages do not establish the practical completeness of bulk export, migration support, or exit rights for institutional customers.
- The exact preservation provider and contractual obligations should be checked for each deployment.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

Figshare shows that a useful repository can be operated commercially while exposing public content and APIs. The lesson is not that commercial operation is inherently incompatible with public benefit; rather, governance, contract terms, preservation responsibilities, portability, and accountability must be explicit.

**Preliminary assessment:** A useful comparator for repository service delivery and commercial sustainability. Its institutional contract model and preservation commitments require more scrutiny than public product descriptions alone can provide.
