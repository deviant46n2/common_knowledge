# Project Profile: Dryad

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://datadryad.org/
- **Operator:** Dryad, a nonprofit membership organization
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

Open data publishing and curation service intended to make research datasets discoverable, citable, and reusable across disciplines. Dryad's public documentation describes human curation of submissions and DOI assignment.

## Main capabilities

- Accepts datasets across fields and file formats.
- Curators check submitted files and metadata and may contact submitters with guidance.
- Assigns a DOI to each published dataset.
- Supports dataset descriptions and subject metadata.
- States preservation practices including checksums, replication, and succession planning.

## Technical and operational model

Dryad illustrates that data publication is more than file hosting: curation, metadata, documentation, rights, and preservation processes affect whether a dataset can be reused. Its policies describe technical curation and preservation procedures; they do not amount to a guarantee that every dataset is scientifically valid or free of sensitive information.

## Strengths

- Human curation and submitter support.
- Community-led nonprofit membership model.
- Explicit preservation and succession policies.
- Dataset-focused workflow and persistent identifiers.

## Limitations and risks

- Curated deposit may require more effort and may not suit every research object or workflow.
- Access, fees, and partner arrangements should be checked for each user context.
- Curation cannot replace research ethics review or expert assessment of scientific claims.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

Dryad is a strong case for examining the operational work behind trustworthy data reuse. Its curation and succession policies are useful comparison points for Common Knowledge's data stewardship and continuity research.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
