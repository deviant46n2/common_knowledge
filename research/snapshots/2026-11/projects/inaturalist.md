# iNaturalist — Project Profile

- **Category:** Biodiversity observation, community identification, and citizen science
- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Initial profile; data licensing and quality rules require project-specific review
- **Primary site:** https://www.inaturalist.org/

## What it is

iNaturalist is an online community and mobile platform where people record observations of organisms, share photographs or sounds, and help identify what they have observed. The community's identifications and observation-quality process produce a subset of records designated “Research Grade.”

Research-grade observations with eligible licenses are exported to the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), making a subset of community-generated observations available through a major biodiversity data infrastructure.

## What role it fills

iNaturalist links public participation, community identification, structured observations, and downstream scientific data reuse. It is not simply a project directory or a one-off volunteer task platform: participants contribute observations over time, and the community helps assess identifications and data quality.

## Strengths and potentially relevant design choices

- Provides an accessible way for people to contribute biodiversity observations.
- Uses community identification and explicit quality criteria rather than treating every upload as equally reliable.
- Connects eligible observations to GBIF through a documented export process.
- Supports learning and community participation alongside data generation.

## Limitations and risks

- Research Grade is a platform-specific quality classification, not a guarantee that every taxonomic identification or observation is correct.
- Only records meeting export criteria and carrying eligible licenses are included in the GBIF dataset.
- Geographic participation, observer effort, detectability, taxonomic expertise, and licensing choices can create biases in the resulting data.
- Exact observation coordinates can be sensitive for threatened species or private locations; location obscuring and other safeguards matter.
- Photographs, sounds, and observation metadata may have different licensing terms; users should not assume all associated media is freely reusable.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

iNaturalist is a strong case for studying how a community can generate structured evidence at scale while retaining a visible role for human review. It also demonstrates the importance of clear quality labels, provenance, licensing, and explicit data-export rules.

## Assessment

**Preliminary assessment:** A compelling model of community-generated, quality-filtered scientific observations. Its model is domain-specific and should not be generalized without considering expertise, sampling bias, licensing, and the sensitivity of location data.
