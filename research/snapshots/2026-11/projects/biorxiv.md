# Project Profile: bioRxiv

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://www.biorxiv.org/
- **Operator:** openRxiv; founded by Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

bioRxiv is a free online archive and distribution service for unpublished preprints in the life sciences. Its purpose is to make manuscripts available before journal publication and enable feedback and earlier communication.

## Main capabilities

- Hosts unpublished life-sciences manuscripts.
- Makes preprints publicly available before journal peer review.
- Performs basic screening for issues such as non-scientific or offensive content, potential health risks, dual-use concerns, and plagiarism.
- Explicitly states that posting does not imply endorsement of methods, assumptions, conclusions, or scientific quality.

## Technical and operational model

A preprint server is a rapid dissemination service, not a peer-review journal. Screening is not equivalent to validation. Readers must be able to distinguish preliminary findings from peer-reviewed or otherwise independently assessed results.

## Strengths

- Free dissemination for authors.
- Speeds communication and enables feedback before journal publication.
- Clear distinction between posting and scientific endorsement.
- Operated by openRxiv, a nonprofit organization.

## Limitations and risks

- Preprints have not undergone formal peer review before posting.
- Screening is necessarily limited and cannot guarantee correctness or safety.
- Life-sciences scope and policies are discipline-specific.
- Long-term funding and governance deserve separate review.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

bioRxiv is relevant to a layered research record in which early claims can be shared without being mistaken for established findings. Common Knowledge should consider explicit status labels, links to later versions, review history, and correction mechanisms.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
