# arXiv — Project Profile

- **Category:** Scholarly preprint and e-print repository
- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Initial profile; organizational transition is current and requires follow-up
- **Primary site:** https://arxiv.org/

## What it is

arXiv is a long-running open repository for scholarly preprints and e-prints, especially in physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering, and economics.

It provides rapid public dissemination of research manuscripts before or alongside formal journal publication. Submissions undergo moderation and screening, but posting on arXiv is not equivalent to journal peer review.

## Organizational transition in 2026

Cornell announced in June 2026 that arXiv would begin a transition to an independent nonprofit organization. Cornell described the change as a response to the financial and logistical demands of operating a large international repository and the need for more stable funding and organizational flexibility.

The Cornell announcement reported that the Simons Foundation International was providing major financial backing for the transition. This is a significant continuity milestone, but it also raises questions about the new organization's governance, funding diversity, institutional relationships, and safeguards against undue influence.

The transition should be followed through the new organization's legal and governance documents, audited financial statements, board composition, and published service commitments as they become available.

## Strengths

- A widely used and discipline-defining preprint infrastructure.
- Supports rapid dissemination and public access to scholarly manuscripts.
- Established moderation and subject classification processes.
- Persistent records and stable identifiers support citation and discovery.
- Long institutional history and community reliance.

## Limitations and open questions

- Preprints are not necessarily peer reviewed; readers must distinguish moderation from scientific validation.
- Subject coverage, moderation practices, and submission policies vary by category.
- The organizational transition creates a need to examine governance, funding, staffing, and continuity arrangements.
- AI-generated submissions and automated content raise new moderation and trust challenges, as discussed by Cornell's announcement.
- This initial profile does not yet analyze arXiv's technical architecture, moderation appeal process, or detailed financials.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

arXiv demonstrates the public value and systemic importance of durable, specialized scholarly infrastructure. Its 2026 transition is especially relevant to Common Knowledge's institutional design: critical knowledge infrastructure needs diversified support, transparent governance, and credible continuity plans as it grows beyond its founding host.
