# Project Profile: Zenodo

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://zenodo.org/
- **Operator:** CERN and OpenAIRE
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

General-purpose repository for research outputs, including datasets, software, publications, and other materials. Records receive DOIs, support versioning, and can be openly available or restricted where appropriate. Zenodo also supports preservation of GitHub software releases.

## Main capabilities

- Assigns a DOI to each published record and supports version-specific and concept-level DOI citation.
- Supports versioning so updated records can be cited individually while the overall work retains a concept DOI.
- Supports open and restricted-access records.
- Provides GitHub integration for archiving software releases.
- Operated by CERN with OpenAIRE involvement.

## Technical and operational model

The DOI versioning model is especially relevant to reproducibility: a citation can point to the exact version used, while a concept DOI can identify the evolving work. Repository deposit does not itself guarantee that data are valid, ethically shareable, or independently reproducible. Users remain responsible for licenses, metadata, and access restrictions.

## Strengths

- Version-specific DOI and concept DOI support traceability.
- Broadly useful general-purpose repository.
- CERN data-centre infrastructure and OpenAIRE partnership.
- Restricted access offers an alternative to indiscriminate public release.

## Limitations and risks

- Centralized service and infrastructure dependency.
- Deposits still depend on contributor-provided metadata and lawful sharing.
- Preservation assurances and service continuity should be evaluated against current policies, not assumed to be permanent.
- Repository storage alone does not supply peer review or methodological validation.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

Zenodo is a useful existing preservation and citation layer. Common Knowledge should investigate interoperability with repositories rather than duplicate general-purpose deposit services. Versioned records and persistent identifiers are particularly relevant to linking claims to exact research artifacts.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
