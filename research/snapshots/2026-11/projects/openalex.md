# Project Profile: OpenAlex

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://openalex.org/
- **Operator:** OurResearch / Impactstory, Inc. (US nonprofit corporation per its public transparency page)
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

OpenAlex is an openly accessible catalog and knowledge graph of scholarly works, authors, institutions, venues, funders, and research concepts. It aggregates metadata from sources including Crossref, DataCite, PubMed, and repositories, then links records into a connected scholarly graph.

## Main capabilities

- Provides a searchable website, API, and downloadable data products.
- Aggregates metadata from many upstream sources.
- Connects works with authors, institutions, venues, topics, funders, and citations.
- Public transparency materials describe the data as CC0 and the code as open source.
- Provides bulk access and API access with a mix of free and paid service tiers.

## Technical and operational model

OpenAlex is not primarily a repository that hosts original research files. It is a discovery and metadata aggregation layer. Its usefulness depends on the quality, coverage, deduplication, and correction of upstream metadata. A record's presence does not certify the quality of the underlying research.

## Strengths

- Openly reusable metadata and open-source code, according to its transparency statement.
- Broad cross-disciplinary aggregation.
- API and bulk data access enable independent analysis and downstream tools.
- Makes links between separate repositories and publication venues easier to navigate.

## Limitations and risks

- Metadata aggregation inherits gaps and errors from source systems.
- Entity matching and deduplication can be uncertain.
- A catalog is not a substitute for access to full text or underlying data.
- Current pricing, data terms, and service limits should be rechecked before building dependencies.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

OpenAlex is a potential discovery and linking layer for a knowledge commons. Common Knowledge should study its metadata model, API, coverage, correction pathways, and license, and consider consuming rather than rebuilding a large scholarly index.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
