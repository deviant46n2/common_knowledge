# Project Profile: eLife

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://elifesciences.org/
- **Operator:** eLife Sciences Publications, Ltd.
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

eLife is a life-sciences and medicine publisher that has experimented with a publishing model in which papers selected for expert review are published as Reviewed Preprints together with public reviews and an editorial assessment, rather than receiving a conventional accept/reject decision after review.

## Main capabilities

- Authors submit directly or transfer a preprint from bioRxiv or medRxiv.
- Editors select submissions for expert review.
- Reviewed Preprints include the manuscript, public reviews, an eLife Assessment, and an author response when available.
- Authors may revise and later choose to finalize a Version of Record.
- eLife publishes its current publication fee and says it is waived for those unable to pay.

## Technical and operational model

This model makes peer review outputs more visible and separates expert assessment from a single binary acceptance decision. It remains a particular publisher's model in defined subject areas, not a universal consensus. Selection for review still involves editorial judgment, and the model's effects on hiring, funding, and scholarly recognition require ongoing assessment.

## Strengths

- Public reviews and editorial assessments accompany the reviewed manuscript.
- Allows readers to inspect strengths, weaknesses, and evidence assessments.
- Supports revisions and versioned publication.
- Preprint-first workflow can make findings and review feedback available earlier.

## Limitations and risks

- Subject scope is life sciences and medicine.
- Editorial selection remains a gate to the review process.
- Publication fees can be substantial, even with waivers.
- Recognition and adoption across institutions and funders remain important questions.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

eLife is a valuable case study in making evaluation more transparent and treating peer review as a visible contribution. Common Knowledge should study review as an inspectable process, while avoiding the assumption that one publisher's model can be generalized across disciplines.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
