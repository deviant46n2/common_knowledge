# Project Profile: PubPub

- **Snapshot:** 2026-11
- **Profile last verified:** 2026-11-26
- **Official website:** https://www.pubpub.org/
- **Operator:** Knowledge Futures
- **Status:** Active service according to current public documentation
- **Confidence:** High for stated features; independent performance, impact, and long-term sustainability have not been fully assessed

## Overview

PubPub describes itself as an open-source, community-led publishing platform for knowledge communities. It supports collaborative editing, publication websites, submissions and review, discussions, DOI support, content relationships, and exports in multiple formats.

## Main capabilities

- Communities can publish articles, reports, books, learning resources, and other content.
- Supports collaborative editing and configurable roles.
- Includes submission and review workflows, discussions, annotations, and access controls.
- Supports DOI registration and typed content relationships deposited to Crossref.
- Supports export to formats including PDF, Word, Markdown, LaTeX, and JATS XML.
- Publicly describes a free robust version and a nonprofit sustainable business model.

## Technical and operational model

PubPub is an example of community-oriented publishing infrastructure rather than a universal research repository or scholarly index. Its emphasis on communities and flexible publication formats may be relevant to independent and interdisciplinary knowledge communities.

## Strengths

- Community-led publishing model and broad range of publication formats.
- Collaborative authoring and review features.
- Export options support portability.
- Typed relationships can connect commentary, reviews, and supplementary content.

## Limitations and risks

- Claims about the sustainability model are operator statements and need independent verification.
- Long-term portability, governance, service continuity, and current source license require further investigation.
- DOI assignment and publishing workflows do not themselves establish research quality.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

PubPub may be relevant if Common Knowledge eventually supports community-owned journals, reports, or public knowledge collections. It is a candidate for integration or partnership research, not evidence that Common Knowledge should build a publishing platform.

## Assessment and open questions

**Documented fact:** The service's public documentation describes the capabilities summarized above.

**Assessment:** Its place in the research ecosystem is best understood as a specialized layer. Common Knowledge should evaluate interoperability, portability, contributor rights, and operational sustainability before depending on it or proposing a replacement.

**Open questions:** What are the current detailed licensing terms? How are governance and funding decisions made? What are the practical export and migration paths? How do users experience the service across disciplines, geographies, and institutional settings? What independent evidence exists about quality, adoption, and long-term preservation?
