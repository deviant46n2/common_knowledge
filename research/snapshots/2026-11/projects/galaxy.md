# Galaxy — Project Profile

- **Category:** Open computational research platform, workflow execution, and community infrastructure
- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Initial profile; public infrastructure and governance are evolving
- **Primary site:** https://galaxyproject.org/

## What it is

Galaxy is an open, community-driven platform for accessible, reproducible, and collaborative data-intensive research. It provides a web interface through which researchers can access curated tools, construct and run analyses, build workflows, and share analysis histories and outputs.

Galaxy is more than a workflow catalog: it combines software, tool integration, execution environments, public infrastructure, documentation, training, and a global community.

## Community, governance, and scale

The 2026 Galaxy update reports more than 650,000 registered users and around two million analysis jobs per month across major public servers, while noting that usage statistics depend on the services counted. It describes more than 400 contributors over the preceding two years across code, tool integration, documentation, training, and operations.

The update describes several governance bodies, including Executive, Technical, and Community Boards, and regional and disciplinary communities. It also reports a project sustainability report addressing funding, maintenance capacity, contributor distribution, and resilience to personnel and infrastructure changes.

The article records a multiyear transition of the core codebase from the Academic Free License 3.0 to the MIT License. This is a useful reminder that software licensing decisions can evolve and that the license of a platform should be checked at the version and component level.

## Strengths

- Combines execution, workflow construction, provenance, sharing, training, and community support.
- Provides free public servers while allowing other organizations to deploy Galaxy.
- Captures analysis steps and histories, helping users inspect and repeat computational work.
- Supports community-curated tools, workflows, and tutorials across multiple disciplines.
- Has explicit governance structures and a public sustainability discussion.
- Demonstrates that training and community support are part of research infrastructure, not optional extras.

## Limitations and open questions

- Reproducibility still depends on tool versions, external data, reference databases, execution environments, and workflow maintenance.
- Public servers have finite resources and operational constraints; availability and compute policies vary by server.
- A recorded workflow does not by itself validate the scientific assumptions or quality of input data.
- A large registered-user count does not establish equitable global participation or sustained active use.
- Long-term sustainability remains an active challenge even for mature, widely used infrastructure.

## Relevance to Common Knowledge

Galaxy is one of the strongest examples found so far of an integrated research environment built around community contribution and open infrastructure. It offers a concrete model for governance, training, tool curation, reproducibility, and distributed deployments. It also cautions against assuming that a successful platform can be sustained by software alone.

**Preliminary assessment:** A highly relevant model of community-governed computational research infrastructure. It deserves deeper study before Common Knowledge designs any workflow execution, reproducibility, or researcher collaboration platform.
