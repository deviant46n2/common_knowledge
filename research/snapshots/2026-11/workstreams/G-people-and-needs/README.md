## Central question

What do researchers, students, independent contributors, communities, institutions, and the public actually need, and what prevents them from participating or benefiting?

## Scope

Learn from academic, public, private, independent, and community researchers; librarians, data stewards, engineers, educators, funders, volunteers, and affected communities. Examine time, funding, credentials, equipment, connectivity, language, accessibility, trust, recognition, incentives, burnout, and retention. Use ethical interviews and consent-aware research.

## Questions to investigate

- Which problems do people themselves identify?
- Who is underserved or excluded?
- What workarounds are common?
- What motivates sustained contribution?
- Which needs are already met by existing services?
- How do needs differ across disciplines and contexts?

## Expected outputs

- Stakeholder and needs map
- consent-aware conversation notes
- themes and barriers
- comparison with existing services
- validated problem statements and priorities.

### Next steps

1. Identify participants across career stages, disciplines, institution types, regions, and support roles—not only senior academics.
2. Conduct a small set of exploratory conversations using the consent-aware guide; do not imply the sample is representative.
3. Ask about actual workflows, workarounds, barriers, incentives, and risks before describing possible Common Knowledge solutions.
4. Include librarians, research software engineers, data stewards, technicians, community researchers, citizen scientists, and affected communities.
5. Record anonymized findings with permission, separating participant statements from interpretation and open questions.
6. Compare reported needs with existing services and revise the opportunity register; seek ethics review if conversations become systematic research intended for publication or generalizable conclusions.
