# Workstream G — Stakeholder Sampling and Listening Plan

- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Exploratory planning document; no participant recruitment has started
- **Workstream:** [G — People, needs, and the research ecosystem](README.md)

## Purpose

This plan is intended to prevent early conversations from becoming a narrow echo chamber. It proposes a small, diverse discovery round to learn about real workflows and unmet needs. It is not a statistically representative survey and should not be described as one.

## 1. Sampling approach

Use purposive, maximum-variation sampling: seek people with different roles, disciplines, career stages, institution types, regions, and resource conditions. Recruit based on experience with the problem, not agreement with Common Knowledge's mission.

An initial round of roughly 12–20 conversations could be sufficient to expose major differences and refine the questions. This is a planning range, not a statistically justified sample size or a stopping rule. Continue when important perspectives are missing or new themes are still emerging.

## 2. Suggested first-round coverage

| Perspective | Why include it | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| Early-career academic researcher | Often experiences publication, funding, and career pressures directly | Incentives, training, data/code sharing, collaboration barriers |
| Senior or independent researcher | May have a longer view of disciplinary and institutional practices | Research lifecycle, institutional gaps, funding, autonomy |
| Research software engineer | Bridges research questions and software infrastructure | Reproducibility, maintenance, compute, software credit |
| Data steward / repository manager | Understands data lifecycle, rights, metadata, and preservation | Deposit friction, metadata, access, preservation, migration |
| Librarian / archivist | Supports discovery, authority records, and long-term custody | Search, metadata, preservation, community stewardship |
| Statistician / methodologist | Sees design, analysis, and verification problems across projects | Reproducibility, validation, research integrity |
| Citizen-science organizer or contributor | Represents participation beyond formal research institutions | Access, training, contribution quality, recognition, bias |
| Community-led researcher or knowledge steward | Brings perspectives on authority, consent, and benefit | Community protocols, data sovereignty, extractive practices |
| Research participant / patient advocate | Represents people whose data and experiences underpin research | Consent, privacy, access, control, benefit |
| Research funder or program officer | Understands incentives and sustainability | Funding cycles, assessment, open-science requirements |
| Research infrastructure maintainer | Understands operations and technical dependencies | Hosting, security, sustainability, governance |
| Researcher in a lower-resourced setting | Helps identify barriers hidden by well-funded institutions | Connectivity, costs, language, institutional support |

The first round need not include every role, but gaps must be recorded and should guide subsequent recruitment.

## 3. Diversity dimensions to track

Only collect broad, non-sensitive context that is relevant and voluntarily provided:
- Discipline or research area.
- Career stage.
- Role in the research lifecycle.
- Institution type (or independent status).
- Broad geographic region.
- Resource context (for example, access to institutional library or compute support).
- Experience with open data, software, repositories, AI tools, or distributed infrastructure.

Do not infer identity, political views, health, or other sensitive characteristics. Do not collect personal demographics unless there is a clear ethical reason and appropriate protections.

## 4. Recruitment channels to investigate

Potential channels include:
- Research Data Alliance working groups and interest groups.
- Open Science Monitoring Initiative and national open-research networks.
- Research Software Engineer associations and local communities.
- University libraries, data stewards, and research support offices.
- Citizen-science networks and project communities.
- Community archive and Indigenous data-governance organizations, approached through appropriate relationship-building rather than cold requests for knowledge.
- Independent research communities and open-source maintainers.
- Early-career researcher networks and disciplinary societies.

A contact channel is not an endorsement. Do not imply that an organization supports Common Knowledge merely because a person was reached through it.

## 5. Selection and outreach safeguards

- Invite people with critical or skeptical views, not only enthusiastic supporters.
- Avoid over-recruiting senior academics and technology advocates.
- Do not treat a single person as the representative of a whole community.
- Do not ask Indigenous or community knowledge holders to disclose protected knowledge.
- Be explicit that the project is exploratory and has no funding or features to promise.
- Offer asynchronous participation and reasonable time commitments.
- Ask permission before recording, quoting, or attributing.
- Do not publish identifiable comments without explicit approval.
- Keep notes minimal, access-controlled, and retention-limited.
- If the work is intended to become publishable human-subjects research or generalizable findings, seek appropriate ethics review before systematic collection.

## 6. Synthesis method

After each conversation:
1. Record the participant's role and context only as permitted.
2. Separate direct statements from interpretation.
3. Identify concrete tasks, barriers, workarounds, and costs.
4. Record existing tools and organizations that already address the need.
5. Capture contradictions and negative cases.
6. Note what the participant says would make a proposed intervention harmful or untrustworthy.
7. Map each theme to supporting conversations and its limitations.
8. Do not count repeated comments as independent evidence if they come from the same network or institution.

A need should become a candidate opportunity only when there is enough evidence about who experiences it, what they do now, and why existing alternatives are insufficient.

## 7. Suggested first-round success criteria

The first round is useful if it:
- Reveals several concrete workflows in detail.
- Includes people beyond university faculty.
- Surfaces needs that conflict with the project's initial assumptions.
- Identifies existing tools or organizations Common Knowledge should support rather than replace.
- Produces a clear list of unanswered questions and missing perspectives.
- Helps determine whether further interviews, a survey, or a technical pilot is appropriate.

The objective is learning, not validation of the idea.
