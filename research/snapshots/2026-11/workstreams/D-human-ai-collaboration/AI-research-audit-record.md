# Workstream D — Proposed AI Research Audit Record

- **Snapshot:** November 2026
- **Status:** Working proposal for testing; not a standard or adopted policy
- **Workstream:** [D — Human–AI research collaboration](README.md)

## Purpose

This proposal identifies information that could make AI-assisted or AI-led research inspectable after the fact. It is designed to connect to the Workstream C research record rather than create a separate parallel research archive.

The goal is not to publish every private prompt or internal conversation. The goal is to preserve the material decisions, inputs, actions, and outputs needed to understand and verify how research claims were produced.

## 1. Research identity and responsibility

- Project and contribution identifiers.
- Research question and stated objective.
- Human contributors, affiliations where relevant, and roles.
- Responsible human lead or institutional steward.
- AI systems and tools used, with model/provider/version identifiers where available.
- Funding, conflicts of interest, and material external dependencies.

## 2. Task and autonomy specification

For each AI-assisted task:
- Task identifier and research stage.
- Task objective and success criteria.
- Inputs and context supplied.
- Tools and permissions made available.
- Autonomy level: assistive, specialist, workflow participant, delegated agent, or autonomous pipeline.
- Human approval points and escalation rules.
- Prohibited actions or constraints.
- Whether the task was completed, abandoned, repeated, or superseded.

## 3. Model and execution context

- Provider and model name/version or dated identifier.
- API or product version where known.
- System/developer instructions or relevant configuration, where disclosure is permitted.
- Sampling and decoding parameters where relevant and available.
- Retrieval corpus or knowledge-base version.
- External APIs, tools, software packages, and execution environment.
- Date/time of execution and relevant service changes.
- Hardware and compute configuration when it affects results.

If a hosted model is mutable or its exact version is unavailable, state that limitation. Do not imply bit-for-bit reproducibility where the provider cannot supply it.

## 4. Inputs, data, and source lineage

- Source identifiers and URLs or repository references.
- Retrieval time and version or snapshot.
- Dataset identifiers, licenses, and access conditions.
- Preprocessing, filtering, deduplication, and transformations.
- Synthetic data generation, including disclosure and parameters.
- Benchmark selection and rationale.
- Exclusions, missing data, and known limitations.
- Sensitive-data restrictions and approved processing locations.

## 5. Actions and workflow trace

Preserve, where material:
- Tool calls and their inputs/outputs.
- Code written or modified and version history.
- Commands executed and exit status.
- Data transformations and intermediate artifacts.
- Search queries, source selection, and citation verification.
- Experiment configuration and parameter changes.
- Failed attempts, retries, and debugging.
- Human interventions, overrides, and approvals.
- Any changes to the original plan.

A concise event log may be more useful than a raw transcript. Each event should link to the relevant artifact and identify whether it was generated by a human, AI system, or external tool.

## 6. Results, evaluation, and interpretation

- Raw and processed outputs where shareable.
- Metrics and evaluation code.
- Baselines and comparison conditions.
- Test-set or benchmark separation and leakage controls.
- Statistical methods and uncertainty.
- Negative, null, failed, or inconclusive results.
- Independent checks, tests, and validation outcomes.
- Known discrepancies between AI summaries and underlying results.
- Claims supported by each result and the human interpretation.

## 7. Human review and accountability

Record:
- Who reviewed methods, code, data, and claims.
- Which AI outputs were accepted, modified, rejected, or not checked.
- What independent domain expertise was applied.
- Who approved consequential actions and publication.
- Who is responsible for corrections, safety, and follow-up.
- How disagreements between human reviewers and AI outputs were resolved.

Do not treat a human click on “approve” as evidence of substantive review.

## 8. Integrity, privacy, and security

- Privacy and consent review.
- Data-processing and confidentiality conditions.
- Tool permission and sandboxing configuration.
- Security incidents, unsafe outputs, or unauthorized access.
- Model/provider data-retention settings where known.
- Relevant risk assessment and mitigation.
- Access controls for audit artifacts.

Raw prompts or traces may contain personal information, confidential material, unpublished findings, or security-sensitive details. Store them under appropriate controls and publish redacted summaries or hashes when public disclosure would be unsafe.

## 9. Versioning and correction

- Stable identifier for each audit-record release.
- Version and timestamp.
- Links to prior versions.
- Corrections to sources, code, data, or results.
- Changes to model/provider or external services.
- Retraction, withdrawal, or superseding work.
- Reason for each material correction.

The record should preserve history rather than silently overwrite the original workflow.

## 10. A minimal event structure

An event record could include:
- Event ID.
- Timestamp.
- Actor type and identifier.
- Action type.
- Task ID.
- Inputs and outputs (or references).
- Tool/model identifier.
- Parameters/configuration.
- Result status.
- Human review status.
- Provenance links.
- Access and retention classification.

This structure could be mapped to PROV-O and packaged with RO-Crate, but that mapping must be tested before being recommended.

## 11. Proportionality levels

| Level | Suitable context | Minimum audit material |
|---|---|---|
| A — Routine assistance | Language polishing, formatting, low-risk organization | Tool identity, task description, human responsibility; disclosure according to venue rules |
| B — Analytical assistance | Literature synthesis, code suggestions, data cleaning, statistical advice | Level A plus source/input versions, prompts or task specifications, code/data lineage, human checks |
| C — Delegated workflow | Multi-step agent tasks, external tools, experiments, model evaluation | Level B plus tool-call logs, environment, permissions, intermediate outputs, failures, review gates |
| D — Autonomous research pipeline | Agent selects methods, runs experiments, analyzes data, drafts claims | Level C plus complete workflow trace, benchmark/data provenance, independent audit, pre-specified evaluation, explicit accountability |

These levels are an initial heuristic. Risk should depend on the actual task, data, and consequences, not merely the label assigned to an AI system.

## 12. Pilot questions

1. Which fields can be collected automatically without creating false confidence?
2. Which artifacts are essential for independent review?
3. What is the smallest useful record for an AI-assisted literature review?
4. What additional records are required for code generation and experimental execution?
5. How can traces be preserved when an external provider does not expose them?
6. How can privacy, confidentiality, and participant rights be protected?
7. Does the record help reviewers detect benchmark leakage, synthetic data, selection bias, or unsupported claims?
8. What is the burden on researchers and what can be automated safely?
