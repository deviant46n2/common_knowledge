# Common Knowledge Research Library

The Research Library is the project's shared, source-backed record of the field around open science, collaborative research, shared knowledge, and the infrastructure and institutions that support them.

Its purpose is to help Common Knowledge understand the existing ecosystem before deciding what to build. It should make our reasoning inspectable, preserve what we knew at a particular point in time, and help future contributors understand how our decisions were reached.

## Scope

1. Existing projects, organizations, communities, and institutions.
2. Technical infrastructure: repositories, federated systems, distributed computing, research software, AI collaboration, provenance, and reproducibility.
3. Scientific and public collaboration: research workflows, citizen science, independent research, publishing, peer review, and knowledge sharing.
4. Governance and economics: stewardship, funding, contributor rights, licensing, accountability, and institutional durability.
5. Standards and foundational work: open-science frameworks, metadata, data stewardship, preservation, and interoperability.
6. Gaps, unmet needs, risks, and opportunities for useful contribution.

## Research principles

- Prefer primary sources for claims about a project's own design, status, governance, and licensing.
- Use independent sources to corroborate claims and understand real-world performance and reception.
- Distinguish documented facts from interpretation, estimates, and unanswered questions.
- Record publication dates and access dates; a page's current contents may not describe its historical state.
- Do not treat a project's stated ambition as evidence that it has achieved that ambition.
- Do not infer that a project is inactive, unsuccessful, or abandoned merely because public activity is hard to find.
- Record uncertainty and disagreement instead of forcing false precision.
- Respect privacy, consent, sensitive data, and community rights.
- Correct errors transparently while preserving the historical record of dated snapshots.

## Snapshot policy

Each snapshot is a dated research edition with a stated research cutoff. Once published, its conclusions should not be silently rewritten. Corrections and clarifications should be documented in the snapshot's change log; later developments belong in a subsequent snapshot or in the ongoing developments log.

A snapshot is not a claim that every relevant project has been found. Its coverage, exclusions, limitations, and confidence should be explicit.

## How to contribute

Start with the [research program](PROGRAM.md) to find the relevant workstream, then read the methodology and use the appropriate template. Add sources close to the claims they support. If you are unsure whether a statement is verified, label it as a question or assessment rather than presenting it as fact.

The library is a research resource, not an endorsement directory. Inclusion does not imply approval, partnership, or agreement with an organization's positions.
